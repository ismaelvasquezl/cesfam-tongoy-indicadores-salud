1Executive summary

This report describes the recorded activity of the Tongoy CESFAM Mental Health Program between July 2025 and August 2026, based on 9,890 anonymized service records across two sources: the MD sheet (medicine, 1,288 records) and the PS sheet (psychology and social work, 8,602 records). All results describe what was recorded: they do not represent demand, population coverage, schedule availability or clinical quality.

Key findings

  1. Activity concentrates in the morning, peaking at 10:00–10:59 and highest on Thursdays.
    59% of encounters start before 12:00. Thursday is the day with the most recorded activity (especially before 10:00), and the midday block (12:00–13:59) is a cross-cutting trough. Read this as a pattern of recorded supply, not demand.
  2. Each professional category has its own, stable start-time profile.
    Social workers start earliest (mode 08:00–08:59; 67% in the morning), psychologists concentrate at 10:00–10:59 (57% morning) and physicians at 11:00–11:59, with a larger afternoon presence (34%).
  3. Psychology capacity depends on very few professionals.
    In psychology, 3 of 7 professionals account for 82.2% of encounters (the highest-volume one, 32.6%). In social work, 3 of 8 account for 71.4%. Medicine is more distributed (16 professionals, 44.7% in the top three). This is an operational-continuity risk in the event of medical leave or turnover.
  4. Almost half of users have a single encounter in the period.
    45.3% of the 2,452 users have a single encounter; the median is 2 encounters per user. At the other extreme, the top 10% of users account for 46.4% of all encounters.
  5. Medical–psychosocial coordination exists but is a minority pattern.
    454 users (18.5%) have encounters in both MD and PS; 203 (8.3%) combine medicine, psychology and social work. The rest are seen in a single modality, which suggests reviewing internal coordination (without being able to confirm whether a formal referral occurred).
  6. Encounter duration cannot be computed from this dataset.
    The “Encounter Close Time” field behaves as an administrative/block close, not a clinical end: it yields medians of 79 min (MD) and 235 min (PS) and values above 8 hours. All duration analysis is ruled out.
  7. Two of every three records have no usable sector.
    66.3% of services fall under “No information” for sector; among those identified, the Red sector has the largest volume. Local territorial analysis is, for now, only indicative.
  8. Recording by Program and country of origin is incomplete.
    “Program” is missing in 85% of MD and 47% of PS (with accent-encoding problems); “Country of origin” is missing in a third of records. Both limit disaggregation.

Actionable recommendations

  • Record the real end time of each encounter to enable duration and schedule-use analysis.
  • Strengthen sector assignment gradually, without making it a mandatory field before it is internalized, so as not to create access barriers or false operability.
  • Review the operational dependence of psychology and social work on a few professionals (contingency plan for leave/turnover).
  • Compare the recorded time pattern with contracted schedules and unrecorded activities before concluding under- or over-use of any block.
  • Establish a monthly dashboard of activity, continuity and hours using the indicators proposed in section 17.
  • Set up a review of continuity for users with high recorded utilization, as a clinical input for the treating team.
  • Normalize categorical fields at the source (gender, sector, country, program) and export in UTF-8 to avoid fragmentation and mojibake.

Three decisions the data does support

  1. Where to reinforce the time offer: the data clearly shows the blocks and days of highest and lowest recorded activity by category (input for reviewing schedule blocks).
  2. How to mitigate concentration risk: the data identifies dependence on a few professionals in psychology and social work (input for staffing and contingency).
  3. Which fields to prioritize in record improvement: the data quantifies the quality gaps (end time, sector, program) that currently block key management analyses.

Three questions the data cannot answer

  1. How much demand went unmet? The data contains only encounters delivered: it does not record requests, waiting lists or no-shows.
  2. Are low-activity blocks available hours? This cannot be known without contracted schedules, leave, field work and unrecorded activities.
  3. Was care clinically adequate or continuous? This is a services dataset, not a clinical-outcomes one; it cannot judge quality, adherence or appropriateness.

2Objective, scope and ethical safeguards

The objective is to deliver a descriptive assessment of the program to support management and clinical review by the treating team, with a primary focus on the actual service hours of physicians, psychologists and social workers.

Ethical safeguards applied

  • Work exclusively with anonymized data; user IDKEY and identities are never shown or reconstructed.
  • Professionals appear only under pseudonymized codes (MED_, PSI_, AS_); staff names are not published.
  • Individual text from the “Comment” field and individual clinical diagnoses are not reproduced.
  • People are not labeled as “severe”, “non-compliant” or “frequent attenders”; the terms used are “users with high recorded utilization” and “who require continuity review by the team”.
  • No suspension, limitation or denial of care based on frequency is proposed; no individual performance rankings are made.
  • Findings are management inputs, not automatic decisions.

Unit of record

Each row equals one service (the “Type” field is always “Service” and “Quantity” equals 1 except for 4 rows in PS). Therefore, in practice: 1 row ≈ 1 service ≈ 1 encounter. Users are counted by unique IDKEY and professionals by ID_PROFESIONAL. Throughout the report, an explicit distinction is kept between services/encounters, users and professionals.

Warning about “Patient Center”

“Patient Center” identifies the user's enrollment center, not the place where care was provided: hospitals and other CESFAM appear. Tongoy Clinic and Guanaqueros Rural Health Post predominate, but the field should not be read as the site of service.

What the maps show

  • Thursday is the most intense day, especially before 10:00.
  • The midday (12:00–13:59) is a cross-cutting trough across all categories.
  • The Friday afternoon (16:00–17:59) drops sharply.

10Encounter duration

Duration is not analyzed (methodological decision)

The “Close Time − Encounter Time” calculation yields clinically impossible results: a median of 79 minutes in MD and 235 minutes in PS, with a 75th percentile of 4.6 and 8.6 hours and maxima near 15.5 hours. There are also closes earlier than the start (71 in MD, 270 in PS) and missing closes (147 in MD, 648 in PS).

Everything indicates that “Encounter Close Time” records an administrative or block close, not the clinical end of each encounter. In line with the analysis rules, duration is not computed from this source. Enabling this analysis requires recording the real end time of each encounter.

19Recommended additional data

Access and timeliness

  • Request date and program admission date
  • Waiting list and protocol-based priority
  • No-show, reason for cancellation and rescheduling

Continuity and coordination

  • Internal and secondary-level referral
  • Back-referral and care plan
  • Lead professional and clinical meeting

Rurality and territory

  • Locality of residence and distance to the CESFAM
  • Travel time and mode; connectivity barriers
  • Encounter modality and care at the rural health post

Activity and capacity

  • Available, used and contracted schedule hours
  • Protected blocks and community activities
  • Field work, clinical meetings, leave and replacements

Outcomes and user experience

  • Validated clinical instruments and therapeutic goal
  • Progress, case closure and readmission
  • User satisfaction and perceived barriers

Key distinction: management variables (schedule, access, territory) can be added immediately; sensitive clinical variables (instruments, progress) require authorization, data minimization and reinforced safeguarding.

20Methodological appendix

Transformation rules applied (the original dataset was not modified)

  • Text standardization: trimming spaces, collapsing multiple spaces, upper-casing and removing accents to compare categories (gender, sector, country, center, professional).
  • Encoding: mojibake correction in “Program” (latin-1 → UTF-8 re-decoding).
  • ID_PROFESIONAL: generated from the standardized name + professional type; prefixes MED_/PSI_/AS_; internal lookup table not published.
  • HORA_ANALISIS: start time from “Encounter Time” (100% populated); “Encounter Date/Time” has no time component; “Close Time” discarded for duration.
  • Time blocks: detailed hourly (before 08:00 … 18:00 or later) and summarized (early/mid-morning, midday, early/mid-afternoon, evening).
  • Age group: 0–9, 10–19, 20–44, 45–64, 65+; descriptive life-course cut.
  • Sector: Tongoy core (Red/Blue/Green/Tongoy no color), “Other center/external” and “No information” (null, “Not reported”, “Undefined”).
  • Modality: inferred from “Description” (keyword heuristic); an approximation, not a recorded field.
  • MD+PS consolidation: valid because of identical schema and meaning (1 row = 1 service); SOURCE_SHEET is kept for all category/sheet breakdowns.

Distinctions upheld throughout the report

Encounters ≠ services ≠ records ≠ users ≠ professionals ≠ recorded hours ≠ available schedule hours ≠ observed demand ≠ unmet demand. Absence of records does not equal absence of care, of demand, or of schedule availability.

Dataset analyzed: 9,890 anonymized services · 2,452 users · 31 professionals · period Jul 2025 – Aug 2026. A support document for management and for clinical review by the treating team; it does not replace clinical or regulatory judgment.

Prepared with anonymized data from the Mental Health Program, Tongoy CESFAM (Chile). Professionals are identified by pseudonymized codes; no names or IDKEY are exposed. Results describe recorded activity and are management inputs, not automatic decisions or individual clinical judgments.
