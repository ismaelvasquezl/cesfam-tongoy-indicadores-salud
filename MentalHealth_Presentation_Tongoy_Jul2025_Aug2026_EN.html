Scope of the analysis · 02

What this report is —and is not—

Before reading a single figure: establish what this dataset can and cannot support.

This analysis describes

The activity actually recorded in the system.

  • Volume and composition of encounters by discipline.
  • Distribution of recorded supply by hour and day.
  • Aggregate profile of the people seen and their continuity.
  • Points of operational dependence and data quality.

This analysis does not measure

Without other sources, these questions are out of scope.

  • Real demand or unmet need in the territory.
  • Population coverage (there is no validated denominator).
  • Schedule availability or idle capacity.
  • Clinical quality or people's health outcomes.

Ethical safeguards applied: work is done only with anonymized data; professionals are identified by codes (MED_/PSI_/AS_) whose key is kept separately; no individual comments or diagnoses are reproduced, and no person is labeled.

Methodology · 1 of 2 · 03

From spreadsheet to reliable data

Four steps turn two spreadsheets into an analyzable, traceable dataset.

1
Single, verified source
Two sheets from the institutional record: MD (medicine, 1,288) and PS (psychology and social work, 8,602). Period, 25-column structure and the absence of hidden sheets were validated.
2
Cleaning and standardization
Case, accents and spaces were unified; sectors and categories written in different ways were consolidated; encoding problems were fixed. This stops the same value from being counted twice.
3
Professional pseudonymization
Each professional received a stable code by category (MED_/PSI_/AS_). The report never shows names; the lookup is delivered in a separate, access-restricted file.
4
Defined unit of analysis
Each row corresponds to one service with quantity 1: for this report, 1 row ≈ 1 service ≈ 1 encounter. Users are counted by anonymized identifier (IDKEY).

Methodology · 2 of 2 · 04

Four principles that frame the reading

Explicit rules so the figures are read rigorously and without over-reading.

A
The record is not reality
Every figure describes what was recorded. Absence of a record does not equal absence of care, demand or availability.
B
Denominator discipline
Coverage or gaps are not asserted without a validated population denominator. Where there is none, the result is declared only indicative.
C
Each unit in its place
Encounter, service, user and professional are counted separately and not mixed. One user may generate many encounters.
D
Honest exclusion: duration
The “close time” behaves as an administrative close, not a clinical end. That is why encounter duration is not computed and is excluded from the analysis.

Data quality · 13

Transparency about the limitations

A good report states its weaknesses; these are the ones that condition the reading.

!
Encounter duration: not computable
The close time works as an administrative close (medians of 1.3 to 3.9 h). All duration calculation is excluded until the real end time is recorded.
◧
Incomplete territorial sector (66% with no usable data)
Limits sector analysis to an indicative level and without denominators.
◧
Social gender mostly “not disclosed” (71%)
Sex is available; social gender does not allow robust segmentation.
≈
Encounter modality inferred, not recorded
In-person / remote / home are inferred from the description text; it is an approximation, not a formal field.

Synthesis · 14

Prioritized findings for management

What the leadership team should take away from this analysis.

1
Activity is morning-based with sharp peaks (Thursdays and 10:00 h)
A solid basis for planning slots, reinforcements and room coordination by block and day.
2
Strong dependence on a few professionals in psychology and social work
Continuity risk with absences or turnover; back-up planning is advisable.
3
High share of single contacts alongside a high-use core
45% attend once; the top-10% users account for 46%: input for reviewing continuity and scheduling.
4
Medical–psychosocial coordination exists, not yet verifiable as formal coordination
18.5% with both disciplines; referral and care-plan recording is still missing to confirm it.
5
Two recording gaps condition everything: duration and sector
Resolving them immediately raises the program's analytical capacity.

Recommendations · 15

A plan across three horizons

From immediate recording fixes to medium-term analytical capabilities.

Immediate
0–3 months · recording
  • Record the real end time of each encounter.
  • Standardize and require the user's sector at intake.
  • Distinguish in the system the modality (in-person / remote / home).
Short term
3–6 months · operations
  • Match slots and reinforcements to the blocks and days of highest recorded demand.
  • Plan back-up for the disciplines with high dependence.
  • Review as a team the high-use core and the single contacts.
Medium term
6–12 months · capabilities
  • Add referral and care-plan fields to measure coordination.
  • Build validated denominators by sector (true coverage).
  • Institutionalize this report as a regular dashboard for management.

In closing

What this dataset
still cannot answer

Unmet demand
How many people need care and do not receive it.
Actual availability
How much schedule is free versus how much is used.
Clinical appropriateness
Whether the care provided resolves the health problem.

This report is a management-support document: it rigorously describes what was recorded and transparently states its limits. Decisions on scheduling, staffing and continuity also require the treating team's view.
